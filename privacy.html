Who we are

Chasely is operated by Duncan Perucatti, South Africa ("we"). Contact: your email address. We are the "responsible party" under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) for information about our customers, who are bookkeepers. For the information bookkeepers load about their own clients, the bookkeeper decides what is collected and why, and we process it on the bookkeeper's behalf.

What we collect

  • From bookkeepers: name, email address, business name, password (stored only in scrambled form) and billing details, which are handled by our payment provider.
  • About a bookkeeper's clients: name, email address and/or phone number, and a list of documents the bookkeeper is waiting for.
  • Messages: the reminders we send, replies we receive, delivery status, and the time each was sent.

We do not store the documents themselves, and we never ask anyone for bank passwords or login details.

Why we use it

Only to run the service: sending document reminders on a bookkeeper's behalf, showing progress, alerting the bookkeeper when a client has not responded, and sending summaries and account emails.

Who else sees it

We use a small number of providers to run Chasely: a hosting provider, an email delivery provider, and, if enabled, an AI provider that helps word reminders. We share only what each needs to do its job. We do not sell personal information.

Opting out

Every reminder includes a way to stop receiving them. A client who replies STOP is paused and receives no further reminders.

How long we keep it

For as long as a bookkeeper's account is active. A bookkeeper can remove a client at any time, and we delete a closed account's data on request.

Your rights

You may ask to see, correct or delete personal information we hold about you, or object to its use. Email us at the address above. You may also contact the Information Regulator of South Africa.

Security

Passwords are stored scrambled, traffic is encrypted, and access is limited to the people who need it. No system is perfectly secure, and we will tell affected people and the Information Regulator if there is a breach that requires it.
